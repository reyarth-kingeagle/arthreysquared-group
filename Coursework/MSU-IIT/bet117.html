Course overview

What you will learn and why it matters.

BET 117 introduces technological change, innovation and engineering practice in society. The course combines group-topic presentations, guided discussions, individual recitations, quizzes and a final sustainable technology case study.

2Lecture hours / week
18Course weeks
9Group presentation topics
3Course outcomes
CO1 · ANALYZE

Technology evolution and impact

Analyze how technology and innovation evolve, and how they affect engineering practice and society using credible evidence.

CO2 · EVALUATE

Benefits, risks and responsibility

Evaluate applications in terms of benefits, limitations, ethics, professional duties, public safety and security.

CO3 · CREATE

Sustainable, feasible solutions

Propose an appropriate technology for a defined community or industry need, considering technical, social, economic and environmental impacts.

01Introduction to Technology & SocietyWeek 2 · Group 1 · CO1+

Explore the relationship among technology, engineering, society and community development.

  • Science, engineering and technology
  • Technology as a response to societal needs
  • Impacts of infrastructure and communication

Think about it: How can a new infrastructure technology create both benefits and unintended consequences?

02Industrial Revolution: Industry 1.0–4.0Week 3 · Group 2 · CO1+

Trace the transitions from mechanization and electricity to digital systems and connected automation.

  • Steam power and mechanized production
  • Electrification and mass production
  • Computers, automation, AI and IoT

Think about it: What changed in construction work between Industry 2.0 and Industry 4.0?

03Innovation Process & Technology Life CycleWeek 4 · Group 3 · CO1+

Understand how ideas become useful technologies and how adoption changes over time.

  • Research, testing, implementation and feedback
  • Introduction, growth, maturity and decline
  • Diffusion of innovation and adopter categories

Think about it: Why might a construction company delay adopting a promising new technology?

04Digital & Information AgeWeek 5 · Group 4 · CO2+

Evaluate digital tools that change communication, information access and engineering practice.

  • Internet, wireless networks and cloud computing
  • CAD, digital simulation and BIM
  • Digital divide, security and information quality

Think about it: What could go wrong if a construction team uses an outdated cloud-hosted drawing?

05Ethics & Professional ResponsibilityWeek 6 · Group 5 · CO2+

Examine responsible engineering decisions when safety, quality, cost and deadlines conflict.

  • Public safety, competence, honesty and accountability
  • Codes, material verification and supervision
  • Environmental stewardship and ethical dilemmas

Think about it: What should a technologist do when pressured to conceal a failed material test?

06Technology Governance, Privacy & CybersecurityWeek 8 · Group 6 · CO2+

Study how organizations protect technical work, personal data and connected infrastructure.

  • Copyright, patents, licensing and intellectual property
  • Data privacy and lawful information processing
  • Cyber threats and protection of operational systems

Think about it: Why could a cyberattack on a water utility affect public safety?

07Sustainable Engineering & Green TechnologiesWeek 9 · Group 7 · CO3+

Explore ways to conserve resources while delivering safe and useful engineering services.

  • Circular economy and responsible materials
  • Green infrastructure, watershed protection and wastewater
  • Life-cycle thinking and sustainability trade-offs

Think about it: How would you evaluate whether a recycled construction material is suitable?

08Emerging Technologies & Smart SystemsWeek 10 · Group 8 · CO3+

See how digital monitoring and geographic information can support safer infrastructure.

  • AI, sensors and smart city operations
  • GIS, remote sensing, LiDAR and flood modeling
  • Automation, predictive maintenance and digital twins

Think about it: What data would be necessary before proposing a smart flood-warning system?

09Technology for Local & Global DevelopmentWeek 11 · Group 9 · CO3+

Connect technology decisions to real community needs and sustainable development.

  • United Nations SDGs and localization
  • Society 5.0 and human-centered technology
  • Appropriate technology, inclusion and implementation

Think about it: Is a low-cost, maintainable community solution sometimes better than the most advanced system?

Final case-study project

Weeks 13–18 · Research, propose and defend.

Work with your assigned group to investigate a real community or industry need. Your case must evaluate evidence, compare examples and propose a sustainable technology application that could realistically be implemented.

  1. Define the need. Identify the community, project or industry problem.
  2. Research the context. Explain technology development and credible technical evidence.
  3. Compare two cases. Assess relevant real-world or local applications.
  4. Evaluate responsibility. Address benefits, limitations, safety, ethics and environmental risks.
  5. Propose a solution. Justify suitability, feasibility, resources, costs and safeguards.
  6. Present and document. Prepare the manuscript, references, individual contributions and oral defense.

Paper checklist: Introduction; Historical/Technical Context; Comparative Cases; Responsible/Ethical/Environmental Evaluation; Technology Recommendation and Feasibility; Conclusion; References.

Course-syllabus format: 5–7 pages, APA 7 referencing, and at least five credible sources. Every member needs identifiable technical contributions. The instructor will confirm submission arrangements.

Common student questions

No. This public learning page does not request registration, student IDs, passwords or personal information.

Can I submit quizzes or examinations here?

No. This page is a public study guide only. Follow your instructor's official instructions for graded quizzes, exams and project submissions.

Group files are not automatically public. The instructor will share approved materials only after checking accuracy and appropriate permissions.

Is there a written final exam?

No separate conventional written final is listed in the syllabus. The final case-study paper and oral presentation are the culminating assessments.
